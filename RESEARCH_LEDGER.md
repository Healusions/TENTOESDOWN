# Research Ledger

## Paul V. McNutt and the Machine

- McNutt was born in Franklin, studied at Indiana University and Harvard Law School, served in the Army during the First World War, and rose through the American Legion.
- As governor he consolidated 169 state agencies into eight departments under the Executive Reorganization Act.
- His administration was associated with the Two Percent Club, through which patronage employees contributed to the Democratic organization.

Primary Indiana reference:

- https://www.in.gov/governorhistory/by-year/paul-vories-mcnutt/

Status: historical foundation. The constitutional break with Washington, disputed Guard order, and 1936 convention are alternate history.

## Frank McHale and Wendell Willkie

- McHale was McNutt's lawyer and political organizer. Active prose treats him as the manager of county chairmen, delegates, and patronage rather than an economic planner.
- Willkie was born in Elwood, attended Indiana University and its law school, worked for Firestone, practiced utility law, and became president of Commonwealth & Southern.
- His public dispute with federal utility policy gives the fictional McNutt government a reason to seek his lawyers, credit contacts, and national credibility.

Primary Indiana institutional references:

- https://www.repository.law.indiana.edu/exhibit/maurer-history/1900-1950/wendell-lewis-willkie/
- https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/wendell-willkie/

Status: historical foundation. Willkie's recovery commission and working arrangement with McNutt are alternate history. He remains outside McHale's party organization.

## Terre Haute, 1935

- Federal Labor Union No. 19694 at Columbian Enameling and Stamping participated in the dispute preceding the Terre Haute general strike.
- McNutt declared martial law and deployed the Indiana National Guard.
- Eugene V. Debs's house on North Eighth Street supplies a real institutional address.
- Powers Hapgood was an Indiana miner and labor organizer.

Status: historical foundation. The prolonged occupation, Wabash Congress, arms routes, militia names, and civil war are alternate history.

## Stephenson and Pelley

- D. C. Stephenson's disclosures were tied to the exposure of Indiana Klan political corruption.
- The Indianapolis Times won the 1928 Pulitzer Prize for its campaign against Klan influence.
- William Dudley Pelley founded the Silver Legion in 1933 and operated from Asheville, North Carolina.

Status: historical foundation. Pelley's copied county index and Indiana takeover are alternate history.

## Harrison and the 1816 Society

- William Henry Harrison and Benjamin Harrison connect the Harrison name to Indiana political memory.
- The Columbia Club was a major Indianapolis Republican institution.
- Indiana's constitutional tradition restricted public debt and executive tenure.

Status: mixed. The 1816 Society, Chicago consortium, Continuity Act, regency, and crown are deliberate inventions. The active opener therefore describes the Regency Council plainly instead of implying an undocumented historical organization.

## Consortium Credit and the Hammond conflict

The Chicago holding company, utility-account schedule, Hammond freight board, and federal enforcement operation are alternate-history institutions. Their procedures borrow from recognizable bank clearing, public-utility filings, freight certification, and emergency military administration, but no active prose presents the incident as a real event.

The conflict is designed as a limited border war that transfers no territory. The four-year United States penalty represents investigations, appropriations fights, resignations, and public doubt following a failed federal operation; it is a gameplay consequence, not a historical claim.

## Purdue physics

- Karl Lark-Horovitz transformed Purdue's Physics Department beginning in 1929.
- Purdue decided in 1935 to construct a Van de Graaff accelerator and a cyclotron.
- Hubert Yearian and John Howe began cyclotron magnet construction in 1936.
- Purdue's shops and faculty labor kept construction costs unusually low.

Primary Purdue references:

- https://www.physics.purdue.edu/about/history/lark_horovitz_era.html
- https://www.physics.purdue.edu/about/history/war_period.html

Status: historical foundation. The procurement trail, state custody choice, and connection to Indiana's foreign strategy are alternate history.

## Culver and the Black Horse Troop

- Culver's mounted program dates to 1897 and became known as the Black Horse Troop.
- Culver maintained extensive equestrian facilities and a summer cavalry tradition; the school also supplied ceremonial escorts for presidents and governors.
- Active prose treats Culver as an instructor, classroom, staff, and mobilization institution. Cadets are not converted into a fantasy combat division.

Primary Culver references:

- https://www.culver.org/culver-life/horsemanship
- https://www.culver.org/about/news-media/cannon/post-details-page/~board/news-stories/post/culver-cannon-story-black-horse-troop-remembered

Status: historical foundation. The Indiana Guard courses and motor-transport translation are alternate history.

## Peru and Hagenbeck-Wallace

- Hagenbeck-Wallace began operating in 1907, grew into one of the largest American circuses, and wintered in Indiana.
- Peru's circus economy preserved specialized rail loading, workshops, animal care, tents, power equipment, and the schedules required to move a traveling city.
- The focus arc uses that expertise for quartermaster instruction and railway construction before ending in a civic revival at Circus City.

Primary Indiana references:

- https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/find-historical-markers-by-county/indiana-historical-markers-by-county/hagenbeck-wallace-circus-1918-circus-train-wreck/
- https://www.in.gov/library/collections-and-services/indiana/subject-guides-to-indiana-collection-materials/circus-in-indiana/

Status: historical foundation. State employment of former circus trainmasters is alternate history.

## Indiana military institutions

- Fort Benjamin Harrison was established in 1903 and became an important infantry, administrative, and finance-training post.
- Camp Atterbury, Jefferson Proving Ground, and major Indiana ordnance installations were historical Second World War facilities.
- The mod's independent Indiana surveys and constructs Atterbury and Jefferson early. Their sites and functions are historical; the 1938 state timetable is alternate history.

Primary Indiana references:

- https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/fort-benjamin-harrison/

## Manufacturers, power, and headquarters

- Allison's Indianapolis operation developed the V-1710 aircraft engine during the 1930s; military acceptance came in 1937.
- Studebaker's South Bend plants grew from the company's long vehicle-manufacturing history.
- Boone County's rural electric cooperative energized Indiana's first cooperative line in July 1935.
- Ball Brothers made Muncie a major glass-manufacturing center.
- The American Legion established its national headquarters in Indianapolis and dedicated its headquarters building in 1925.
