# Research Ledger

## Historical foundations used in the active opening

### McNutt government and the Two Percent Club

- Paul V. McNutt became governor in 1933 and used the Executive Reorganization Act to consolidate Indiana administration.
- Frank McHale was a central political organizer in McNutt's Machine.
- State employees were expected to join the Hoosier Democratic Club and contribute two percent of salary.

Status: historical foundation. Exact offices and conversations in events are dramatized.

### Terre Haute, 1935

- Federal Labor Union No. 19694 at Columbian Enameling and Stamping participated in the dispute preceding the Terre Haute general strike.
- McNutt declared martial law and deployed the Indiana National Guard.
- Eugene V. Debs's house on North Eighth Street provides a real institutional address for the route.
- Powers Hapgood was a labor organizer and Indiana native.

Status: historical foundation. The Wabash Congress, relief network, arms route, and civil war are alternate-history inventions.

### Stephenson and Pelley

- D. C. Stephenson's papers and disclosures were tied to the exposure of Indiana Klan political corruption.
- The Indianapolis Times won the 1928 Pulitzer Prize for its campaign against Klan influence.
- William Dudley Pelley founded the Silver Legion in 1933 and operated from Asheville, North Carolina.

Status: historical foundation. Pelley's acquisition and use of copies from Stephenson's surviving political network is an alternate-history construction.

### Harrison Crown and the 1816 Society

- The Harrison family connects Benjamin Harrison V, William Henry Harrison, and President Benjamin Harrison to Indiana political memory.
- The Columbia Club was a major Indianapolis Republican institution.
- The Constitution of 1851 constrained Indiana debt and limited governors to four years in any eight.

Status: mixed. The 1816 Society, Chicago holding company, guarantor structure, Continuity Committee, regency, and crown are deliberate alternate-history inventions.

### Purdue physics

- Karl Lark-Horovitz transformed Purdue's Physics Department beginning in 1929.
- Purdue decided in 1935 to construct a Van de Graaff accelerator and a cyclotron.
- Hubert Yearian and John Howe began cyclotron magnet construction in 1936.
- The Van de Graaff was completed in early 1937; the cyclotron was completed in August 1938.
- Purdue's shops and faculty labor kept construction costs unusually low; the recorded cyclotron cost was $9,030.90 excluding much labor.

Primary institutional references:

- https://www.physics.purdue.edu/about/history/lark_horovitz_era.html
- https://www.physics.purdue.edu/about/history/war_period.html
- https://www.physics.purdue.edu/about/prizes_awards/horovitz.html

Status: historical foundation. Indiana's use of the accelerator program to negotiate an end to the federal standoff is an alternate-history invention.
